Projects

Essence of Crystals, a calmer way to shop and learn

A website redesign for a small crystal and holistic goods shop, built around clear education, easy browsing, and a checkout that stays out of the way.

Role

UX/UI Designer

Context

September 2023-May 2024, KVStudios Client Project

Tools

Figma

Focus

Research Wireframing UI Design
Overview

A shop built on healing, with a site that did not show it.

The Essence of Crystals sells crystals, jewelry, and holistic tools, and shares what it knows through a community of customers, supporters, and a podcast. The goal was a website that feels as calm and trustworthy as the products, and that helps people learn about crystals as easily as they can buy them.

I managed the project from start to finish. I researched the competition, mapped how different customers move through a crystal shop, then designed the experience from low-fidelity wireframes to a finished, high-fidelity design in Figma.

Competitors

Three shops, three different problems.

I compared Crystal Age, Energy Muse, and Crystal Magic on their mission, audience, strengths, weaknesses, and four key features: education, video, accounts, and product previews.

Each one fell short in a different way. Crystal Age had a huge range but felt overloaded and hard to trust. Energy Muse offered the most education, but its pages were text heavy and did not match its look. Crystal Magic had beautiful photography but little to teach. That left room for a site that is calm, uncluttered, and education-first.

Research

Three customers, three journeys.

To understand how different people shop for crystals, I built a journey map for three customers: a 25-year-old college student curious about holistic healing, a 40-year-old corporate worker looking for calming products after a stressful day, and a 60-year-old holistic expert reviewing products for trustworthiness.

Across all three journeys, the same frustrations kept showing up: too much information at once, unreliable sources, no way to preview products on a person, and little reason to come back, like accounts or rewards. Each map ends with opportunities that fed straight into the design.

Wireframes

Getting the structure right first.

Before choosing colors or photos, I sketched the core pages in grayscale: the home page, the shop, and checkout, plus the add-to-bag and thank-you moments. Working without visual style let me focus on what customers see first and how few steps it takes to buy.

Design

Soft, warm, and easy to read.

The visual style uses a warm cream background, soft lavender bands, and muted tan buttons, with natural photography of crystals, candles, and herbs. Rounded cards and generous spacing keep every page uncluttered, which was the biggest gap in the competitor sites.

The home page opens with a single hero message, then three clear paths: New Arrivals, Best Sellers, and a Holistic Guide. A quiz banner near the bottom helps visitors who are not sure what they need.

Education

Learning made easy to browse.

Every journey map pointed to the same fix: condense the information and add ways to learn. The Learn page breaks everything into six topics with photography, so customers can pick what interests them instead of reading everything at once. The About page tells the founders' story, which helps the shop feel real and reliable.

Shopping

Preview first, then buy.

The shop uses a category list and a clean product grid with a heart to save favorites. Selecting a product opens a quick preview with a short description and quantity, so customers do not have to leave the page to decide. Adding to the bag confirms the choice and offers View Bag or Keep Shopping, plus recommendations for what to look at next.

Checkout

A short path to the finish.

Checkout keeps the product, the order summary, and the accepted payment methods on one screen. The summary shows subtotal, shipping, and tax up front, so there are no surprises, and recommended items sit below for one last look. A short thank-you message closes the loop.

Conclusion

What I learned.

This project showed me how much a competitor review and a few simple journey maps can shape a design before any screens exist. Seeing the same frustrations across very different customers made it easier to decide what to simplify, what to teach, and where to build trust. It also gave me practice managing a client project from first research through finished screens.
